# Opportunity Analysis

## Findings

After listening to Oscar talk about Eco Resurf, one of the biggest things I noticed was that they already use AI, but there are still a lot of areas where their work is done manually. They currently use tools like Microsoft Copilot, Grammarly, and Google NotebookLM for things like research, brainstorming, organizing information, and improving their writing. Even with these tools, Oscar mentioned that things like proposals, certifications, reports, and other documents can still take a lot of time.

I think the biggest opportunity for Eco Resurf is not having AI replace what their consultants do, but instead using it to make some of these repetitive tasks easier. The three areas that stood out to me the most were creating executive summaries and reports, helping with certification documents, and helping prepare proposals.

## Evidence From the Partner Visit

One example Oscar gave that really stood out to me was a project they did for a large local government. Eco Resurf had around a 25-page report along with an Excel file that had multiple worksheets. Oscar wanted to take all of that information and turn it into a simple one-page document that an executive could look at and quickly understand.

He tried using Copilot and Google NotebookLM to help create it. The problem was that even though AI helped create the document, it was difficult to actually edit and use. After trying a few different versions, Oscar ended up recreating it himself. I think this is a good example of where AI could help Eco Resurf, but also shows that just because AI creates something doesn't mean it actually saves time.

Another area that stood out was certifications like LEED. Oscar explained that certifications usually follow a pretty clear process. There are requirements that need to be met, calculations, documents that need to be collected, information that has to be submitted, and comments that may come back for changes. Since some of these steps repeat from project to project, I think AI could help organize documents, keep track of requirements, or help with first drafts.

At the same time, Oscar made it clear that he doesn't want AI making the important decisions. Their consultants still need to use their own experience and knowledge when making recommendations for clients.

## Analysis

Based on what we heard, I would start with the executive summary idea. Oscar already gave us a real example where this was a problem, so we know there is at least some need for it. The goal would be for the consultant to still decide what information and recommendations are important, while AI helps turn that information into something shorter, clear, and easy for the client to understand and edit.

My second opportunity would be helping with certifications. This could possibly save a lot of time because the process has repetitive steps. My third would be proposals, since Oscar also mentioned that they are still pretty manual.

The biggest thing to remember is that AI should support the consultants instead of trying to replace their expertise.

## Open Questions

There are still some things I would want to learn before deciding which opportunity is best. I would ask Oscar which of these tasks takes his team the most time each month. I would also want to know exactly what went wrong with the one-page summary he tried to create and which part of the certification process takes the most time.

Another important question is what AI tools they are actually allowed to use. Oscar mentioned that some clients restrict certain AI tools, so whatever solution we recommend has to work with their privacy and client requirements.

Getting answers to these questions would help us figure out which AI opportunity would actually save Eco Resurf the most time and be realistic for them to use.
