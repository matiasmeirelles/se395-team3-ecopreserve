### LEED Certification Process Walkthrough

Matias: What's the process of onboarding a new customer? Walk us through the last time that you prepared a certification document. What takes the longest?

Oscar: Because we have so many different types of projects, certifications have a very predictable process for the most part. The other ones are a lot more fluid.

Oscar: The U.S. Green Building Council started a program to create some standards of what a green building was. And they called it LEED, Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design. Right now we're on LEED version 5, and we at ecoPreserve help organizations meet the standards to get the certification. You can get it at a certification level, or you can have three other ranks if you want to stretch yourself.

Oscar: We usually start at the design. It's supposed to be an integrated process. So we help our clients from the very beginning where they're in conceptual design. Once they start schematic design, when they start to actually do some wireframing, what the building could be, how it is positioned, what is outside, how the user experience will be, then we get involved.

Oscar: Our involvement usually is to help them understand what they need to do from beginning to end, create a roadmap, give them clarity on the landscape, what they're signing up for. Oscar: So we help them make those decisions. There's a lot of documentation that supports those decisions, calculators that need to be populated, for the most part, manually. There is an organization called Green Badger that has advanced the automation of that process, and they do a pretty good job with that.

Oscar: So once we get involved, we start documenting what the building needs to do, we create some narratives to describe the strategy that they want to use, and then we start documenting information and pulling a portfolio of proof that the building is compliant during the design phase. Oscar: From a LEED perspective, design is about 40% of the journey because most of the decisions are made at design. And construction is mostly about verification. And then we do it again.
