# ecoPreserve: AI Usage

## Current Tools

| Tool | Use Case |
|------|----------|
| Microsoft Copilot | Primary tool. Research, drafting, refinement across Microsoft suite |
| Grammarly | Leveling communication quality across consultants |
| Google NotebookLM | Research integration, working with large data sets and multiple sources |
| BidPrime | AI-enhanced aggregator for government solicitations. Four pages of keywords, embedded AI bot for summarizing solicitations |

## Workflow Pattern

AI for initial research and idea dumping, human expertise for recommendations and value-add, AI again for refinement (clarity, brevity, removing redundancies).

## Key Gap: Document Formatting and Iteration

Oscar described a concrete failure: tried to create a one-page executive summary of a greenhouse gas inventory using NotebookLM. The template came as PDF only, edits were clunky, repeated refinement made it worse. He ended up opening multiple queries, picking the best design elements from each, and manually assembling the final product. Export to Google Slides and PowerPoint was also problematic.

## Solicitation Search

BidPrime's keyword filters are too broad. Four pages of keywords still yield hundreds of irrelevant results. The new AI bot helps summarize solicitations without opening documents, but the filtering and relevance scoring is still largely manual.

## AI Stance

No "sacred cows." ecoPreserve views AI as an enhancer, not a replacer. They don't delegate leadership or recommendations to AI but welcome it for research, structuring, and refinement.

Some government clients explicitly prohibit AI use on their projects (must sign a form confirming no AI was used).

## Staffing Model Shift

During the pandemic, ecoPreserve flipped its staffing model. Went from mostly junior consultants with 1-2 seniors to exclusively senior consultants who power their work with AI. No junior associates remain.

"A lot of the research that we used to delegate to junior associates is no longer available to them."
